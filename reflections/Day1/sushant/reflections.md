# Day 1 Reflections

**When I first saw the problem statement, I thought the real task was...**
To figure out how to actually train metacognition, that is to build a tool that forces you to slow down, notice gaps in your own understanding, and simplify the problem before handing it off to AI tools.

**The first thing I personally wanted to do was...**
Build an engine around decomposition of a vague problem into concrete subtasks. Startin from a vague idea, the tool pushes back, asking you to break it down into concrete pieces before anything gets handed off to an AI. This will prevent the act of passively prompting and letting the AI run amok and do it all for us without gaining anythinh from it.

**The first thing our team actually did was...**
To discuss about what we were actually trying to achieve. There were many different views on whether the goal was to make people better at using LLMs, or to protect the thinking that they'd otherwise skip.

**That helped because...**
It helped me gain a cleaner direction, towards coming up with a second agent and to incorporate questionnaires and confidence ratings. These ideas came after we finalized the general user interaction flow.

**That hurt because...**
We spent a lot of day one on that conversation when we could have been prototyping. There's also a risk that you never fully settle it and just keep circling back.

**One assumption I made about teenagers, learning or AI was...**
That the user already wants to learn and not get queries answered passively. And, they should have decent AI literacy and domain knowledge since the tool does not teach those.
