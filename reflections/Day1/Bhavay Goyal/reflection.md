# Day 1 Reflection: Navigating Ambiguity and AI

**When I first saw the problem statement, I thought the real task was...**
When I first looked at the problem, I had 2 interpretations in my mind. One was like building a tool in a way that helps the government to teach the kids all the relevant skills they need in an AI-driven worls which looked extremely broad. Other was to build an hands-on actual tool that forces people to think while using AI, instead of just copy-pasting the answers.

**The first thing I personally wanted to do was...**
Focus on one of the specific problem based on what I've seen in my real life related to the problem statement. I've noticed most students just copy their assignment into an AI, get the answer, and move on which has literally 0 learning, 0 thinking. I wanted to build a tool which on asking the question does not directly tell you the solution but instead the tool will cross-question you in a way that forces the user to actually think by giving you hints, and slowly make you reach the final solution.

**The first thing our team actually did was...**
We started by talking about the problem statement, trying to understand different opinions we had about the problem statement itself. Than talked through a bunch of different product ideas that we all had in our minds. Instead of jumping right into coding or asking Claude what to make, we debated different angles. For example, one big idea was to make a space with broken, pre-written code that the user had to debug and the tool will slowly help the student correct it through hints and widgets.

**That helped because...**
Talking through these totally different ideas helped us actually understand the problem statement. It made us think hard about what "learning by doing" really means and helped clear up the confusion from the prompt.

**That hurt because...**
Having so many different discussions kept us brainstorming for a long time. It was a good discussion, but having too much discussions probably wasted some of our time where we could have made some further progress.

**One assumption I made about teenagers, learning or AI was...**
That the student using the tool actually wants to learn. I based my idea on the guess that users genuinely want to understand the concepts and get better at thinking, rather than just looking for the fastest way to finish their homework.
