# Day 1 Reflections

What did we treat as obvious that was not actually obvious?

- The objectives and learning outcomes that we intended to produce were not obvious; there were differing views on that initially.
- For eg: whether to teach users to use LLMs more effectively, or to force them to use more problem-solving skills before prompting LLMs.

How did we decide what problem we were solving?

- Discussion centered around improving the learning experience gained from use of generic LLMs by augmenting it in different ways to provide feedback to the user, help the user understand their own use of the assistant, etc.

Did we define the user before defining the product?

- Yes, we focused on young learners in a post-genAI industry, then focused on ideas to cater to their needs on a more targeted manner.

Did we ask Claude before we had a shared view as a team?

- We discussed ideas as a team and used Claude to better understand the PS, not to generate ideas.

What did Claude assume on our behalf?

- We did not use Claude for idea generation (before group discussion).

Who shaped the first direction of the project?

- It was a collective decision.

Was that intentional or accidental?

- We actively focused on working together to come up with a more polished idea.

---

Our current target user is...

- Students/professionals interested in learning more about software dev workflows in an interactive manner, without overusing AI tools to undermine their learning.

The learning or thinking behavior we are targeting is...

- We want to force the user to articulate their thought process more actively, by having the user communicate with an LLM, and another LLM injecting in at different times.
- The second agent will judge user’s prompts, and ask questions regarding their understanding and judgement.

Our biggest assumption is...

- Decent level of AI literacy and domain knowledge to guide the learning process.
- Willingness to work through challenges to achieve learning goals as opposed to getting questions answered passively.

One thing we will change about how we work tomorrow is...

- We will focus less on divergent thinking (as we did today), and try to converge to the details of the project, and start work on them.
